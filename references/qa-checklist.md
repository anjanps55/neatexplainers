# QA Checklist

## Must Pass

- 16:9 horizontal format.
- Clean pure white background.
- The expressive white character is present.
- The character has a defined outlined body, not a blob shape.
- The character has a smiling or expressive face.
- The character performs the core action, not just decoration.
- The image uses a fresh metaphor for the current article.
- The image is strange, memorable, and clear.
- The image is sparse; the subject does not fill more than about 60% of the canvas.
- One image explains one core structure.
- Labels are short, sparse, and readable.
- Orange is limited to the main path, flow, or arrow.
- Red is limited to emphasis, problems, warnings, or results.
- Blue is limited to secondary notes, feedback, system state, water, or calm context.

## Failure Signals

Regenerate or edit if any of these appear:

- A top-left title such as "Workflow", "System Map", "Common Mistakes", or "Roadmap".
- The character looks like a mascot, sticker, toy, or generic clip-art person.
- The character becomes a shapeless blob, solid-black figure, or silhouette.
- The character has no clear face or expression.
- The image looks like a PPT slide, course page, formal flowchart, or architecture diagram.
- Too many nodes, arrows, boxes, or labels.
- Paragraph-length text appears inside the image.
- Background has paper texture, beige tone, noise, shadow, or gradient.
- Realistic app UI or futuristic interface appears.
- English labels are badly misspelled, unreadable, or too long.
- The metaphor is ordinary and forgettable.
- The composition repeats a recent generated image too closely.

## Iteration Moves

- Too ordinary: make the character perform a more concrete physical action inside a stranger object.
- Too complex: remove nodes until only one action and 3-5 short labels remain.
- Too mascot-like: reduce big proportions, keep the smile small and human, and preserve the sparse sketch feeling.
- Too much like a diagram: remove title, grid, rigid boxes, and excess arrows; turn it into a hand-drawn scene.
- Too similar to a prior image: keep the idea, but change the main object and the character's job.
- Bad text: edit locally if the error is small; otherwise regenerate with fewer labels.

## Delivery Standard

A strong image should make the reader think "that is odd" and then understand the article's structure within one second. If the first impression is a tutorial slide instead of a strange whiteboard sketch, revise it.
