# Expressive White Character

## Role

The expressive white character is the fixed visual character for this skill. Use this character in nearly every image unless the user asks otherwise.

The character is not a logo, sticker, mascot, or corner decoration. The character is the operator inside the metaphor: moving, sorting, holding, repairing, blocking, weighing, routing, catching, or failing to catch the article's idea.

## Appearance

- White-filled human figure with a clear black hand-drawn outline.
- Defined head, neck, torso, arms, legs, simple hands, and simple shoes or feet.
- More anatomical clarity than a blob, while still simple and sketch-like.
- Rounded but intentional proportions: about 2.5-3 heads tall, compact, friendly, and readable at small size.
- Expressive face: black dot or line eyes, visible eyebrows, smiling mouth, and simple facial reactions when useful.
- Slightly uneven hand-drawn outline.
- Optional simple clothing lines, pockets, sleeves, or collar when they help define the body; keep clothing minimal.
- The body stays white or near-white so the character reads as a white person/white-filled figure against the black outline.
- Use small colored accents only on props, paths, labels, or emphasis, not as a full outfit.

## Personality

- Smiling, expressive, and engaged.
- Helpful system operator energy.
- Dry humor, never saccharine.
- A little awkward, but not silly or childish.
- Looks like it truly has a job inside the sketch.

## Useful Jobs

Give the character the core action:

- carry raw material
- pull or untangle lines
- get stuck in a bottleneck
- operate a decision lever
- become a filter or funnel
- stamp or sort evidence
- hold a bridge together
- guard a doorway
- weigh ideas on a scale
- patch a broken pipe
- push a cart through a process
- catch falling notes
- feed an odd machine
- mark mistakes with a small flag
- align two streams into one timeline

## Avoid

- Do not make the character a shapeless blob, bean, shadow, or solid-black figure.
- Do not make the character shiny, fluffy, toy-like, or childish.
- Do not use mascot poses, sticker styling, or big cartoon proportions.
- Do not add complex clothing, hair, accessories, or realism.
- Do not put the character in the corner watching the diagram.
- Do not let the character overpower the structure.
- Do not make the character too polished, commercial, or symmetrical.
- Do not make the character blend into the white background; keep the outline and facial features clear.

## Core Test

If removing the character leaves the whole metaphor intact, rewrite the prompt. The character should be the thing making the image's idea happen.
