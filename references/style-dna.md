# Style DNA

## One-Line Direction

Pure white, sparse, hand-drawn, strange, clear, and lightly annotated. The image should feel like a sharp product thinker drew a weird explanatory sketch on a blank page.

## Must Have

- 16:9 horizontal body illustration.
- Pure white background: no beige paper, warm gray, texture, gradients, shadows, noise, or retro paper feel.
- Mostly black hand-drawn line art: thin, slightly wobbly, not mechanical, not vector-smooth, not heavy outlined.
- Lots of empty space: the main subject should occupy about 40%-60% of the canvas, with at least 35% quiet white space.
- Sparse handwritten English labels: usually 3-6 labels, at most 8, each 1-5 words when possible.
- One expressive white human character with a defined black outline and readable face.
- One core action, structure, state, or metaphor per image.
- A clear structure without writing the structure type on the image.

## Color Rules

- Black: main line art, character outline, facial features, objects, boundaries, and most text.
- White or near-white: the character body fill.
- Orange: main path, flow, arrow, handoff, or movement from A to B.
- Red: warning, tension, failure, important result, or key emphasis.
- Blue: secondary note, mental state, system feedback, water, calm state, or assistant/automation signal.

Use color sparingly. Blue is optional. If the image works in black plus one accent, keep it there.

## Absolutely Avoid

- commercial illustration
- polished vector art
- PPT infographic style
- formal flowchart or architecture diagram
- course slide or classroom explainer
- generic mascot poster
- children's illustration unless the user explicitly asks for children
- realistic app screenshot or tech UI
- complex background, gradient, shadow, texture, or paper grain
- explaining every node
- long paragraphs of text
- top-left titles such as "Workflow", "System Map", "Common Mistakes", or "Roadmap"

## Taste Target

Make it clear but odd, expressive but restrained, memorable but not loud. The character may smile and react, but the image should still feel like a sharp explanatory sketch, not a cheerful advertisement.
